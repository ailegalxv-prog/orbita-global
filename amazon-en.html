Sales models

How we sell on Amazon

We list well-known brands from Amazon suppliers and buy each item only after a shopper has ordered it. An intermediary warehouse is needed from day one: parcels arrive there, get repacked and are shipped to shoppers. We start with the model that brings the first sales and gradually add the ones with higher margins.

Launch · first sales in months 2–3

Joining existing listings

The product already has a listing on Amazon in that region, with photos, a description and reviews. We add our offer at our price, and the system keeps price and stock up to date every day. No content needs to be created, which is why this model brings the first sales right after the month of preparation.

Second stage

Creating new listings

We list products in regions where they are not yet available. For each listing we prepare content: we source or generate photos and write the title, description and specifications. A listing of our own faces less competition, and we set the price.

In parallel, after launch

Direct suppliers

×2…×5+margin

We negotiate better purchase prices directly with the suppliers of brands that already sell. The margin can be several times higher than at launch: 2, 3, 5 times or more, depending on the agreements and volumes.

How it scales

The system and the team grow together

The system takes over the routine: it collects data, calculates prices and updates stock every day. But the more products and brands there are, the more people are needed so that each person controls their own area. The team grows with the catalogue and sales, and its growth is paid for by the store's profit.

Why these markets

Mexico and Canada first

This is where competitors already earn with this model: one of them makes 64% of its sales in Mexico. One Amazon seller account opens access to several marketplaces, so the next market does not require a new store from scratch. Brazil is still an open question: we are studying the restrictions currently in place there. Next we expand to other marketplaces: Amazon operates in almost twenty countries.

What the market shows

Competitors grow by quantity, not quality

The largest competitor has more than 1.6M inactive or incomplete listings. The smaller competitor gets mass suspensions from adding products without a system. Both problems are solved with entry filters and daily control.

Our approach

Fewer products, better selection

We enter with brands checked for demand, trademarks and restrictions. Every listing passes a filter of banned words and categories. A healthy account lets us open new brands faster than competitors.

Who does what

The technical team and the brands and account protection team

A system that pushes prices and stock for thousands of products every day stores large volumes of data and has to be configured and indexed properly. That is why the technical team has two people from day one: a team lead and a technical assistant, so everything runs without interruption seven days a week. Brands, legal matters and account health are handled by a separate team.

Technical team · lead and assistant

Data, listings, updates, access

01

Data collection from Amazon suppliers. Regularly collects prices, stock, photos and specifications for every product.

02

Data storage. The system stores millions of records on products and prices. It has to be configured and indexed properly to run fast.

03

Price calculation. Automatically calculates the price for each marketplace using the agreed pricing rules.

04

Filters. Technically checks every product against the lists of banned words, brands and categories.

05

Product listings. Joins offers to existing listings in bulk and creates new ones with prepared content through Amazon's programmatic access, and fixes upload errors.

06

Daily updates. Makes sure prices and stock in the store match the supplier every day. Otherwise orders get cancelled and items sell at a loss.

07

Access. Keeps the system's access to the accounts. Amazon programmatic access keys must be renewed every 180 days, otherwise the system stops.

Brands and account protection · team lead

Brands, legal support, account health

01

Market and brand analysis. Selects brands with demand by sales rank and checks restrictions and hazardous goods.

02

Opening brands. 5–10 new brands per week: trademark checks, invoice preparation, applications for approval to sell.

03

Pricing rules. Sets the pricing rules together with the technical team and decides which brands and products to list.

04

Restriction review. Reviews products awaiting approval every day. Opening a brand takes 7–10 days.

05

Account health. Checks the metrics twice a day. If the account health rating drops below 200, Amazon blocks opening new brands.

06

Legal support. Appeals against listing blocks, review of rights-holder complaints, trademarks, invoices and documents. If needed, a brand is paused temporarily.

07

Instructions and new markets. Prepares instructions for registering the company and the store and helps launch on each new marketplace.

Marketing

Ads bring many orders

The first sales come from well-chosen products and prices. Already in months two and three we launch ads inside Amazon for the products that sell and grow them together with the catalogue. That way every advertising dollar works on a proven product.

Access

Working with accounts without interruptions

The system and the team work with the account every day, so access is handed to the responsible people and maintained continuously. The technical team is responsible for programmatic access, the brands and account protection team for account health.

Delivery

From order to shopper

Delivery speed affects the store's rating. That is why from day one a separate person on our team handles purchasing: buys the item, makes sure it reaches the intermediary warehouse and is shipped to the shopper on time. The intermediary warehouse (for example, Western Bid in Miami) does not buy goods: it receives our parcel, repacks it and quickly ships it to Mexico, Canada or another market.

Order

The shopper pays for the item on Amazon; the purchasing manager receives the order.

Purchase

The manager buys the item from the supplier with delivery to the intermediary warehouse.

Warehouse

The intermediary warehouse receives our parcel, repacks and labels it without delay.

Shipping

The warehouse quickly ships the parcel to the shopper in Mexico, Canada or another market. The tracking number is passed to Amazon, and the manager monitors delivery.
